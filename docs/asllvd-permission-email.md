**To:** carol@bu.edu
**Subject:** Permission request: ASLLVD sign clips in a free student accessibility project (SunHacks 2026)

Dear Professor Neidle,

I am a student at Arizona State University building **Day One** at the SunHacks hackathon. It is a free, non-commercial web app that helps teenagers and first-generation students see what a day in a job looks like and practise job interviews. Accessibility for Deaf and hard-of-hearing students is a core goal: every screen has captions, and we would like the narration and interview questions to be available in American Sign Language.

Our pipeline turns each short English sentence into ASL gloss and plays the matching sign clips in sequence. We would like your permission to use individual sign clips from the American Sign Language Lexicon Video Dataset (ASLLVD) for this, specifically:

- **What:** about [200–400] individual citation-form sign clips (camera 1), chosen from the vocabulary in our scripts. We would not use or host the full session videos.
- **How they are shown:** clips are played inside the app, joined into short sequences. They are never offered for download, and the app is free with no ads or paid features.
- **Where:** a hackathon demo on [dates], and a public demo site afterwards (we can keep it non-public if you prefer).
- **Credit:** the app would name ASLLVD, link to http://www.bu.edu/asllrp/, http://dai.cs.rutgers.edu/ and http://dai.cs.rutgers.edu/dai/s/signbank, and cite Neidle, Thangali & Sclaroff (2012) and Neidle et al. (2022) in the app and our project write-up.
- **Honesty:** the app will state that sentences are assembled from individual citation-form signs, are not natural ASL grammar, and have not been reviewed by a Deaf signer.

If this is not something you can allow, I completely understand, and I would be grateful for any suggestion of a dataset or resource that would be appropriate.

Thank you for making this resource available to researchers and educators.
